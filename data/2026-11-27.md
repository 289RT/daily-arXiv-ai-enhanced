*Xiangting Li,Tom Chou*

Method: 开发了一种新的粗粒度随机模型，结合蛋白质足迹大小和构象转换，通过拟合RPA与ssDNA的结合动力学数据验证模型。

Abstract: In many canonical biophysical and biochemical contexts, complex diffusing DNA-binding proteins undergoing nonspecific adsorption and desorption can exist in different conformational states. Equilibrium binding theories and kinetic models of competing ligands provide a basis for describing these systems. Closed-form, tractable mathematical models that accurately describe the stochastic dynamics of structured proteins that bind to and diffuse along DNA are lacking. Here, we develop a new coarse-grained stochastic model of nonspecific DNA-protein binding in the fast diffusion regime, from which we derive an effective Markov model and its corresponding mean-field approximation that incorporates protein footprint size and conformational switching. Finally, by including multisegment binding states, our analysis captures facilitated exchange, revealing how multisegment architecture balances high affinity with rapid remodeling. Our theoretical model applies broadly to many other DNA-binding protein systems, including nucleosomes, ssDNA-binding proteins, and other multisegment complexes, providing accurate predictions and new strategies for evaluating experimental data.

### [2] [KnitNet: Learning to Search at Inference Time for Exact RNA Inverse Design](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33812)
*Antonia Panescu,Xin Wang,Haoran Xu,Qingyu Chen,Carlos Oliver,Rex Ying*

Main category: q-bio.BM

TL;DR: KnitNet是一种RNA逆设计框架，通过验证反馈优化序列以实现精确折叠匹配，提高搜索效率，适用于多种RNA结构设计任务。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 现有方法可能错过精确结构匹配或需高成本搜索，KnitNet旨在通过学习验证反馈优化序列，解决这些问题。

Method: KnitNet通过不匹配折叠推断核苷酸依赖性，结合在线代理预测，优化修复策略，提高搜索效率和准确性。

Result: 在五种二级结构基准测试中，KnitNet平均提高21%精确匹配率，减少70%求解时间，是首个在OpenKnot57上实现精确匹配的方法。

Conclusion: KnitNet通过适应性优化信号和验证反馈，在不同RNA结构设计中实现高效且精确的逆设计，展示了出色的泛化能力和计算效率。

Abstract: RNA inverse design seeks nucleotide sequences that fold into specified target structures. Existing methods rely on learned generators that can miss exact structural matches, especially beyond their training distributions, or on costly search over a vast, multimodal sequence space. Search is further complicated by globally coupled nucleotide interactions, as correcting one pairing error can create new errors beyond a fixed repair scope. To address this, we introduce KnitNet, an inference-time scaling framework that learns from verifier feedback to refine sequences for exact fold-back matching. Starting from candidates generated for a target secondary structure, KnitNet uses mismatched folds to infer nucleotide dependencies and adapt its joint repair strategy, while an online surrogate learns to predict which edits are promising. This makes search progressively more informed and compute-efficient. Crucially, KnitNet generalizes across RNA structural regimes by changing only the optimization signal to match the verifier required by each setting. Across five secondary-structure benchmarks, KnitNet increases exact solve rates by an average of 21 percentage points over continued sampling with 70% less time-to-solve. On OpenKnot57 specifically, KnitNet is the first method to achieve exact computational matches for all complex, pseudoknotted structures. Beyond 2D, KnitNet improves 3D structural self-consistency over per-target RL by 7.8% while being 39.3% faster, demonstrating generalization and effective inference-time scaling across RNA design regimes.

  <summary>Details</summary>

Method: 使用ab initio密度泛函理论计算GC和AT碱基对的Kohn-Sham分子轨道能级，分析能级间距的概率分布、相邻间隙比、数方差和谱压缩性。

### [5] [From the Deterministic Hook Effect to Stochastic Reliability in Ternary Complex Formation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33753)
*Deepak K. Agrawal*

Main category: math.DS

TL;DR: 研究探讨了三元结合系统在小体积反应中可靠形成复合物的机制，发现即使确定性模型预测有利，但小体积中复合物的形成可能因分子不足或材料分配不均而失败。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 理解三元复合物在小反应体积中形成的可靠性，以揭示平均结合响应与实际组装之间的差异。

Method: 使用确定性方程、精确随机模拟和简单的催化扩展模型研究最小可逆结合网络。

Result: 小体积常无法积累足够复合物；较大体积和适当动力学参数可提高可靠性。

Conclusion: 平均结合响应良好不一定能保证小体积中复合物的可靠组装，需考虑动力学和体积因素。

Abstract: Ternary binding systems occur in signaling, scaffold-mediated assembly, and targeted protein degradation. A mediator brings two partners into a productive ternary complex, but excess mediator can hold them in separate binary complexes. Consequently, ternary complex formation can increase and then decrease as the mediator dose increases, producing the hook effect. Deterministic dose-response curves describe the average behavior but do not indicate whether individual small compartments reliably form the complex. A minimal reversible binding network was studied using deterministic equations, exact stochastic simulations, and a simple catalytic extension. The deterministic model recovered the hook-shaped response. In stochastic simulations, small volumes often failed to accumulate the required number of complexes, even where the deterministic prediction was favorable. At low mediator doses, failure reflected a shortage of mediator molecules; at high doses, material accumulated in free or binary-bound forms. Larger volumes increased the range of mediator doses supporting reliable formation, while stricter copy-number thresholds narrowed that range. Faster ternary-complex completion or slower breakup improved reliability. In the catalytic model, product formation also depended on how early productive complexes formed. These results show why a favorable average binding response does not necessarily ensure reliable assembly in small reaction volumes.

</details>
