### [1] [Dark energy: the cost of function in protein evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27711)
*Ezequiel A. Galpern,Federico Caamaño,Ignacio E. Sánchez,Diego U. Ferreiro*

Main category: q-bio.BM

TL;DR: 论文讨论了蛋白质进化中的“暗能量”概念，即进化能量与物理折叠能量之差，以及如何通过计算和实验方法量化这种能量。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 研究动机在于理解蛋白质进化过程中稳定性和功能之间的冲突及其对进化能量的影响。

Method: 利用深度突变扫描、蛋白质语言模型和逆折叠模型等方法，量化蛋白质宇宙中的暗能量。

Result: 揭示了暗能量的存在及其对从序列到结构再到功能的信息流的影响。

Conclusion: 研究表明暗能量是理解蛋白质进化中功能与稳定性冲突的关键因素，为生物信息流提供了新视角。

Abstract: The evolutionary fate of proteins is driven by both folding stability and biological function, dual constraints that often conflict, creating frustration and imposing functional costs beyond stability. These costs can be captured by a "dark energy": the difference between the evolutionary energy of protein sequences and their physical folding energy. Recent advances in deep mutational scanning, protein language models, and inverse-folding models have enabled the quantification of dark energy across the protein universe. We review the computational and experimental approaches that disentangle folding and function at scale, revealing a dark energy component and providing new insights into how biological information flows from sequence to structure to function and back to sequence.

### [2] [How a polymer filling enhances the rate and selectivity of colloid permeation across mesopores](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27995)
*Mikhail Y. Laktionov,Frans A. M. Leermakers,Ralf P. Richter,Leonid I. Klushin,Oleg V. Borisov*

Main category: cond-mat.soft

TL;DR: 高分子功能化的介孔在胶体分离、传感和递送方面具有潜力，研究发现吸引胶体的聚合物填充反而增加胶体渗透，并提出设计新型介孔设备的策略。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 核孔复合物（NPCs）在生物体中展示了独特的胶体选择性渗透能力，但其物理原理尚不明确，研究者希望揭示这一机制并设计高性能介孔设备。

Method: 通过分析高分子填充介孔的胶体渗透行为，研究聚合物填充对胶体捕获和渗透的影响，并与裸孔进行比较。

Result: 吸引胶体的聚合物填充反而增加胶体渗透，且可选择性控制不同尺寸和表面特征的胶体。

Conclusion: 研究为NPCs的选择性渗透提供了物理解释，并启发了高性能介孔设备的设计。

Abstract: Polymer-functionalised mesopores are an emerging technology for colloid separation, sensing and delivery. Their potential is strikingly illustrated in living cells, where nuclear pore complexes (NPCs) control biocolloid transport between the nucleus and the cytosol. Even colloids much smaller than the biopolymer-filled NPC channel are effectively blocked, but some larger colloids with distinct surface features rapidly permeate. Simplistically, one may expect any polymer filling to obstruct and slow down colloid transport. We demonstrate how a polymer filling that attracts colloids and extends beyond the mesopore, thus maximizing colloid capture, can instead increase permeation compared to a bare pore. We also define how polymer-filled mesopores can effectively gate colloids according to their size and surface features. Our findings provide a basic physical explanation for the exquisite permselectivity of NPCs, and a rational design strategy for novel mesopore-based separation, sensing, catalysis and drug delivery devices with enhanced performance features.

</details>
