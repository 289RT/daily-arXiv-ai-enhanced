### [1] [FastCREST: GPU-Accelerated CREST Conformer Searches with Consistent Energies and Gradients](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28599)
*Supantha Dey,Nilusha L Kariyawasam,Ratul Chowdhury*

Main category: q-bio.BM

TL;DR: FastCREST通过优化硬件使用和GPU加速，显著提升了CREST的构象搜索速度，同时保持了结果的准确性。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 针对CREST在大分子构象搜索中耗时过长的问題，提出FastCREST以硬件优化提升效率。

Method: FastCREST通过改进OpenMP设置、引入GPU加速SCC计算、使用Slurm数组作业并行几何优化等方法优化硬件使用。

Result: FastCREST在多个分子上的搜索速度比传统CREST快2.2到7.1倍，且能量和梯度结果与CREST一致。

Conclusion: FastCREST在保持结果准确性的同时大幅提升了构象搜索效率，适合大规模分子研究。

Abstract: CREST conformer searches with GFN2-xTB take days for large, flexible molecules: one search of a 274-atom gallium(III)--peptide complex with CREST 3.0.2 took 321.5~h on 32 CPU threads. FastCREST keeps CREST's algorithm and changes how a search uses the hardware. Its metadynamics (MTD) and molecular dynamics (MD) trajectories run with tuned OpenMP settings and one GPU, which solves the self-consistent-charge (SCC) eigenproblem and evaluates the three-body dispersion term. About 15,000 geometry optimizations per iteration run as Slurm array jobs on other CPU nodes, and checkpoints let a search continue on preemptible GPUs. On A100 nodes, the OpenMP settings and loop changes made an MTD step 2.4 times faster and the GPU another 2.3 times; array jobs made a batch of optimizations 39 times faster. For six more molecules of 24 to 339 atoms, a FastCREST step was 2.2 to 7.1 times faster than a conventional CREST step. A complete FastCREST search, which also lifts CREST~3's cap of 200 optimization cycles, took 54.3~h, evaluating energies and gradients 8.1 times as fast as the CREST 3.0.2 search (on different hardware) with fewer CPU-hours per evaluation. Two opt-in settings (a warm-started Jacobi eigensolver and GPU-resident SCC iterations), LLVM's OpenMP library and one CPU socket per trajectory made an MTD step 8.3 times faster than conventional CREST. Energies agree with CREST 3.0.2 within $1.01\times10^{-10}$ Eh and gradients within $8.2\times10^{-11}$Eh bohr$^{-1}$, and controlled MD runs passed a prespecified equivalence test. Most replicate FastCREST and conventional CREST searches ended in similar compact conformers, with no detectable difference in energy.

### [2] [General-purpose language models are neurosymbolic reasoners able to annotate proteins from sequence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28529)
*Christopher J. Mungall,Marcin P. Joachimiak*

Main category: q-bio.GN

TL;DR: 前沿通用模型在蛋白质注释任务中表现出色，展现了结合序列、结构和符号推理的潜力。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 探索前沿通用模型是否具备从序列单独进行蛋白质注释的能力。

Method: 为不同前沿通用模型分配多种蛋白质注释任务，包括预测Pfam结构域、功能催化残基的缺失等。

Result: 模型在所有注释任务中表现显著优于随机猜测，但未与专业工具对比。模型存在典型的幻觉误差。

Conclusion: 前沿模型的初步结果表明，结合序列、实验数据和文献知识的神经符号方法可能提升蛋白质功能注释能力。

Abstract: Determining the function of genes across the tree of life is one of the major challenges in biology. Generative AI approaches to this problem generally fall into two categories: language models trained on biological sequences that are able to predict structure and function from signals in those sequences; and generalist language models trained on text, that are able to reason about biological function symbolically. A default operating assumption is that protein language models do not perform well on symbolic tasks, and that generalist language models do not perform well on sequence tasks. However, a recent report showed that frontier generalist models perform surprisingly well on next-residue prediction tasks. We set out to explore whether these capabilities extended to protein annotation.
  We assigned different frontier generalist models a variety of protein annotation tasks, including predicting the presence and coordinates of Pfam domains, loss of functional catalytic residues, and predicting structure and activity. On all annotation tasks, the frontier models performed well above chance; we did not benchmark them against specialised tools. The models also make characteristic hallucination errors, and they state them as confidently as their correct calls.
  Though untested against specialised methods, the nascent ability of frontier models to perform protein annotation from sequence alone is intriguing. The ability to reason over sequence, structure, and symbols in a single model allows for a neurosymbolic approach where a combination of sequence, experimental data and knowledge from the literature can be holistically combined to improve on de novo functional annotation of the protein universe.
  Prompts, test sets, model outputs and code: https://github.com/ai4curation/fable-sequence-to-function. Summary of results: https://ai4curation.io/fable-sequence-to-function/

### [3] [Impact of Topology and Sequence Length on the Pressure-Dependent Collapsibility of Proteins](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30336)
*Jiayi Wang,D. Thirumalai,Margaret S. Cheung,Andrei G. Gasic*

Main category: physics.bio-ph

TL;DR: 该论文研究了蛋白质的“可塌陷性”及其与天然态折叠的关系，特别关注水压对蛋白质塌陷的影响。研究发现，水压会破坏完全折叠的天然态，但仍可能促进紧凑的水介导构象。此外，水压对可塌陷性的影响程度和方向依赖于天然态的拓扑结构。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 探索蛋白质序列形成紧凑构象的内在倾向（可塌陷性）及其与天然态折叠的关系，并研究水压对蛋白质塌陷的作用。

Method: 扩展了蛋白质可塌陷性的理论框架，引入与水压相关的脱溶剂势能，分析水压对蛋白质塌陷的影响。

Result: 研究发现，水压会破坏完全折叠的天然态，但促进紧凑的水介导构象；可塌陷性的变化与天然态拓扑结构密切相关。

Conclusion: 天然态拓扑结构决定了蛋白质塌陷对水压的敏感性，揭示了蛋白质结构、溶剂介导作用与环境扰动之间的相互作用。

Abstract: Several physical and evolutionary factors, including temperature, pressure, solvent quality, and natural selection, influence how amino acid sequences adopt their native structures. Although protein folding has been extensively studied, the physical determinants of protein collapse and their relationship to native-state folding remain less understood. In particular, "collapsibility", the intrinsic propensity of a protein sequence to form compact conformations, is distinct from but closely related to "foldability", the ability to reach the native structure. Previous theoretical studies suggested that collapsibility is encoded in native-state topology, potentially reflecting evolutionary selection. However, the roles of solvent-mediated interactions and hydrostatic pressure remain unclear. Here, we extend the theoretical framework of protein collapsibility by incorporating a pressure-dependent desolvation potential to investigate how hydrostatic pressure influences protein collapse. We find that increasing pressure destabilizes the fully folded native state, but can still favor the compact, water-mediated conformations. Furthermore, the magnitude and direction of pressure-induced changes in collapsibility depend strongly on native-state topology, including secondary and tertiary structures. Proteins and domains with a higher proportion of non-local native contacts generally exhibit greater pressure-induced water-mediated collapsibility. These findings highlight the role of native-state topology in determining the pressure sensitivity of protein collapse and provide insights into the interplay among protein structure, solvent-mediated interactions, and environmental perturbations.

### [4] [RumiFold: Teaching an LLM to Natively Fold Proteins and Reason About Their Function](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29444)
*Kevin Lu,David Bau,Wengong Jin*

Main category: cs.LG

TL;DR: RumiFold是一种预训练语言模型，不仅可以预测蛋白质结构，还能通过折叠后的表示回答自然语言问题，且在蛋白质功能预测方面表现优异。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 探索通用语言模型是否能学习折叠蛋白质，并研究这种学习能力在结构预测之外的潜力。

Method: 通过重复折叠步骤处理蛋白质，利用折叠后的隐藏状态回答自然语言问题。

Result: RumiFold在蛋白质折叠准确性上媲美专用模型，并在功能预测和描述任务中达到先进水平。

Conclusion: RumiFold展示了语言模型与蛋白质计算的集成潜力，为生物分子智能的发展提供了方向。

Abstract: Can we teach a general-purpose language model to fold proteins? And if so, what does learning to fold enable beyond structure prediction itself? We introduce RumiFold, a pretrained chat model that learns to predict protein structures directly from its own hidden states. RumiFold processes proteins through repeated folding passes, then uses these folded representations to answer questions in natural language. It achieves folding accuracy comparable to dedicated single-representation models such as SimpleFold, alongside state-of-the-art performance on Gene Ontology protein function prediction and free-text function description. We find that folding pretraining organizes the model's hidden states around protein function, creating an internal per-residue workspace that is biologically grounded. Causal interventions show that this folded workspace links structure and language when RumiFold predicts function, providing rich representations for biomolecular reasoning. We see RumiFold as a step toward native biomolecular intelligence: where protein-based computations and language-based reasoning become integrated capabilities of general-purpose language models. Code, data, and models are available at: https://rumifold.baulab.info

### [5] [What Do Co-Folding Models Learn? Insights from Enhanced Transition Path Sampling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30062)
*Muhammad R. Hasyim*

Main category: physics.chem-ph

TL;DR: 该论文提出了一种基于扩散的生成模型来预测生物分子复合物结构，并通过过渡路径采样（TPS）技术评估模型学习到的构象分布是否具有物理意义。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 研究目的是理解扩散模型在生物分子复合物结构预测中学到的构象分布是否与其物理统计分布一致。

Method: 引入过渡路径采样（TPS），利用马尔可夫链蒙特卡洛方法在去噪轨迹上采样学习到的分布，并结合增强采样技术恢复完整的构象集合。

Result: 研究发现Boltz-2模型的构象变化偏向于接近训练数据的晶体态，表明模型既学习到了物理规律，也存在记忆现象。

Conclusion: 论文建议通过综合考虑结构恢复、与训练数据的接近程度以及构象集合来评估和改进协同折叠模型。

Abstract: Co-folding models are diffusion-based generative models that sample biomolecular complexes from distributions learned on a large corpus of experimental data. Although these models are powerful for structure prediction, it remains unclear whether the conformational ensembles they learn resemble physically meaningful statistical ensembles. To understand what these models learn, we introduce transition path sampling (TPS), which uses Markov chain Monte Carlo over denoising trajectories to sample the learned distribution. Because its Monte Carlo trajectories resemble biomolecular dynamics, TPS couples directly with enhanced-sampling techniques to recover the full conformational ensemble. Applied to Boltz-2, enhanced TPS reveals physical conformational changes biased toward crystal-like states near the training data, indicating both learned physics and memorization. These results suggest new ways to evaluate and improve co-folding models by jointly assessing structural recovery, proximity to training data, and conformational ensembles.

</details>
