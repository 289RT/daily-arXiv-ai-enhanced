### [1] [ProTEUS: A mutation-resolved world model for closed-loop protein evolution](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13348)
*Yikun Zhang,Minghao Guo,Guangxue Xu,Peter Yichen Chen,Wojciech Matusik,Wengong Jin,Jingjing Chai,Kaiyi Jiang*

Main category: q-bio.BM

TL;DR: ProTEUS是一种基于突变的闭环蛋白质进化模型，通过结合序列、进化和结构信息，快速预测突变功能，并通过实验反馈优化预测，显著提升蛋白质活性。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 蛋白质工程面临复杂的适应度景观，实验筛选仅能覆盖有限序列空间，需高效计算模拟突变组合。

Method: ProTEUS利用MutationFormer架构预训练，学习序列、进化和结构的联合表示；开发快速物理约束原子预测器；通过闭环实验反馈优化预测。

Result: ProTEUS在突变预测任务中超越现有模型，实验验证在醛脱氢酶和蔗糖酶中实现活性提升10倍以上。

Conclusion: ProTEUS为高效蛋白质进化提供虚拟评估与实验学习结合的世界模型框架。

Abstract: Protein engineering requires navigating rugged fitness landscapes, where small sequence changes can produce large functional differences, but experimental screening can only sample a small fraction of sequence space. Thus, a central challenge is to efficiently traverse the large combinatorial space of mutations in silico. We introduce ProTEUS, a mutation-resolved world model for closed-loop protein evolution. Pretrained on deep mutational scanning assays, ProTEUS learns a joint representation of sequence, evolutionary, and structural information using MutationFormer, a mutation-biased attention architecture. Variants are represented as states in this joint latent space, and mutations define actions whose functional consequences are predicted. To support exploration of many mutants, we developed a fast, physics-constrained all-atom predictor that utilizes explicit geometric constraint enforcement to achieve >10 fold increase in speed than AlphaFold3 with guaranteed physical validity. Lastly, ProTEUS refines its prediction and understanding of protein activity through closed loop experimental feedback to guide evolution toward a desired phenotype. ProTEUS surpasses established models in both zero-shot and supervised fine-tuning mutation prediction tasks. We experimentally demonstrate its utility in two closed-loop multi-objective engineering campaigns on aldehyde dehydrogenase and levansucrase, yielding variants with >10 fold higher activity than wild type. ProTEUS provides a world-model framework that connects virtual evaluation of mutational outcomes with experimental learning for efficient protein evolution.

### [2] [ANTIOCH-VS: From Sequence to Hits with Modular, Open-Source Ensemble Virtual Screening](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14287)
*Hale S. Hasdemir,Venkata Sai Sreyas Adury,Pratyush Tiwary*

Main category: physics.chem-ph

TL;DR: ANTIOCH-VS是一个开源框架，用于从头开始进行虚拟筛选，自动生成受体构象并筛选候选配体，无需先验晶体结构。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 针对缺乏配体结合晶体结构的靶点，传统方法难以捕获结合态构象，因此开发了ANTIOCH-VS以自动化完成虚拟筛选流程。

Method: 通过ColabFold和AlphaFold2-RAVE构建构象集合，结合DOCK6或AutoDock Vina进行对接，通过物理元动力学（CTMD）筛选优化结果。

Result: 在JAK2激酶和ADRB2 GPCR的测试中，ANTIOCH-VS的筛选结果与配体结合晶体相当，且显著优于单AF2模型或apo晶体。

Conclusion: ANTIOCH-VS提供了高效且自动化的虚拟筛选解决方案，适用于缺乏晶体结构的靶点，并显著提升了筛选命中率。

Abstract: Structure-based virtual screening needs a receptor conformation that fits the ligand class, but for most targets no ligand-bound crystal is available -- and a single predicted model or an apo structure often misses the binding-competent state. We present ANTIOCH-VS, an open-source framework that runs an entire in silico screening campaign, from a protein sequence to a ranked shortlist of candidate binders, as one plug-and-play workflow. It first builds a conformational ensemble by reduced-MSA subsampling with ColabFold, then uses AlphaFold2-RAVE (AF2RAVE) -- short molecular-dynamics simulations with State Predictive Information Bottleneck analysis -- to pick out docking-ready, Boltzmann-weighted metastable states. It then detects the binding pocket, prepares receptors and ligands, docks the library across the ensemble with DOCK6 or AutoDock Vina, builds a consensus ranking, and triages the top hits by physics-based metadynamics (CTMD). We benchmark it on two very different targets, JAK2 kinase and ADRB2 GPCR, using their DUD-E benchmark sets of known binders and property-matched decoys. Starting from sequence alone -- no ligand-bound crystal, no manual intervention -- the AF2RAVE ensembles gave top-20 shortlists strongly enriched for true binders: a 20% hit rate for JAK2 and 60% for ADRB2 (random ~1-2%), on par with ligand-bound crystals and, for the kinase, above single AF2 models and an apo crystal. The full pipeline runs end to end on a workstation or HPC from a single command, or each module can be run on its own with user-supplied inputs. ANTIOCH-VS is freely available at https://github.com/tiwarylab/antioch-vs

### [3] [Emergent Biological Capabilities in a Foundation Model for Molecular Interactions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14504)
*Adi Mashiach*

Main category: cs.LG

TL;DR: Bonbon是一种用于分子相互作用的基础模型，展示了无需任务特定监督的分层分辨率下的三种零样本能力，并在湿实验室验证中表现出色。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 探索基础模型在多模态生物学中的应用，尤其是分子相互作用的零样本能力。

Method: 使用约3.2万亿个蛋白质-配体序列对训练Bonbon，进行六种模型规模的扩展实验。

Result: Bonbon在功能、残基和键三个层次上展现出零样本能力，且性能随模型规模跃升；湿实验室验证命中率达61.5%。

Conclusion: Bonbon证明了基础模型在分子相互作用中的潜力，并揭示了参数-标记比对零样本能力的影响。

Abstract: Foundation models trained on large unlabeled corpora develop emergent capabilities, tasks that go beyond the training objective. We extend this paradigm to multimodal biology with Bonbon, a foundation model for molecular interactions trained on approximately 3.2 trillion tokens of paired protein-ligand sequences. From a single representation space, Bonbon exhibits three zero-shot emergent capabilities at hierarchical levels of resolution: functional (mechanism of action), residue (binding site localization, including orthosteric/allosteric discrimination), and bond (covalent versus non-covalent engagement). All three capabilities generalize to out-of-distribution complexes. Bonbon was trained on protein-ligand sequences alone, without any task-specific supervision and without structural coordinates. We conducted controlled scaling experiments across six model scales from 42M to 1.6B parameters; every capability evaluated shows a sharp jump at the largest scale on continuous metrics, even as pretraining loss decreases smoothly. This demonstrates that loss-based scaling laws alone are insufficient to predict when zero-shot emergent capabilities become available. The capabilities reported here emerge after training at a 1:2,000 parameter-to-token ratio to accommodate the sparsity of paired biological interaction data. The same frozen representations achieve state-of-the-art binding and affinity prediction at five orders of magnitude greater speed than co-folding methods. In prospective wet-lab validation across nine disease targets spanning seven protein families, Bonbon achieves a 61.5% dose-response hit rate on de novo compounds, yielding structurally novel molecules not previously reported to inhibit these proteins, including for out-of-distribution targets.

</details>
