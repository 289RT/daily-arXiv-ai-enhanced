### [1] [Protein B-factor modeling with local magnitude](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08832)
*Wanying Bi,Jian Liu,Hongsong Feng*

Main category: q-bio.BM

TL;DR: 论文提出了一种局部拓扑量级表示方法，用于保留残基级结构信息，解决了全局方法计算成本高的问题，并在蛋白质灵活性预测中表现更好。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 全局拓扑量级方法在计算大蛋白质时成本过高，需要一种更高效且保留结构信息的新方法。

Method: 引入局部量级表示，基于最近邻环境计算，计算复杂度降低至O(Nk³)。

Result: 实验表明，局部方法在364个蛋白质中的平均PCC略有提升，计算成本大大降低。

Conclusion: 局部拓扑量级是一种紧凑且高效的表征方式，适用于大蛋白质的结构预测。

Abstract: Topological magnitude characterizes the effective size and spatial organization of metric spaces, offering a geometric approach with potential applications in molecular biology and materials science. Previous work has demonstrated its utility in modeling protein flexibility from structure, but global magnitude weighting requires dense linear systems whose computational cost increases with protein size. We introduce a local magnitude representation that retains residue-level structural information through nearest-neighbor environments. The construction is well posed and geometrically invariant, with central weighting distinguished from scalar neighborhood magnitude. Across 364 proteins, the mean within-protein fitting Pearson correlation coefficient (PCC) increases from 0.725 for global magnitude to 0.728 for local magnitude. Blind-prediction experiments on 345 proteins combine each representation with identical auxiliary descriptors and evaluate random forests and gradient boosting under three validation protocols. Local features improve the primary PCC in all six model--protocol comparisons, with a maximum absolute increase of 0.008 in protein-level ten-fold gradient boosting. For a fixed feature bank, local factorization costs $O(Nk^3)$ compared with $O(N^3)$ for global factorization, where $N$ is the number of C$_α$ atoms and $k$ the neighborhood size. Timing measurements show a computational advantage of local magnitude on large structures. Within the tested single-seed configuration and distinct global and local scale banks, these results support local magnitude as a compact representation that preserves useful B-factor information while reducing feature-extraction costs for large proteins.

### [2] [Hybrid Quantum-Classical Annealing for Protein Side-Chain Optimization: A Feasibility Study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08696)
*Valeria B. C. Visani,Simone Reale,Alfonso Gautieri,Elisabetta Di Nitto*

Main category: cs.ET

TL;DR: 该研究发现，D-Wave混合优化器在蛋白质侧链优化这一实际问题上具有竞争力，尤其是在大实例中表现优于经典商业求解器Gurobi。


<details>
  <summary>Details</summary>
Motivation: 验证混合量子-经典退火求解器在二进制二次规划问题上的优势是否适用于实际工业问题，特别是蛋白质侧链优化。

Method: 研究比较了D-Wave混合求解器与商业求解器Gurobi在40个蛋白质实例上的表现，涉及最多2000个二进制变量。

Result: 混合求解器在小实例中与Gurobi相当，在大实例中表现更优，能够找到更低能量的解。

Conclusion: 混合求解器在蛋白质侧链优化等实际问题上具有竞争力，为进一步与经典启发式算法比较提供了动机。

Abstract: Hybrid quantum-classical annealing solvers are moving from research laboratories toward industrial use, but their advantage over classical optimization appears to be confined to a narrow class of problems. A recent systematic benchmark of the D-Wave hybrid solver against industry-leading classical solvers, across several classes of optimization problems, found it to be most competitive on binary quadratic programs, while noting that few industrial problems take this form. Therefore, identifying real-world industrial problems that can be modeled with this structure is a key challenge. We propose protein side-chain optimization, a core step of computational protein design, as one of these problems: it is natively a binary quadratic program, and its application context requires pools of low-energy solutions, which annealers naturally return. In this feasibility study, we test whether the competitiveness of the hybrid solver against industry-leading classical solvers extends to this problem. On 40 instances derived from 20 experimentally determined protein structures with up to about 200 amino acids and 2000 binary variables, we compare the results achieved with a hybrid solver, run for 5 seconds, against those of the commercial solver Gurobi, run for 10 seconds, 1000 seconds, and 4 hours. The hybrid solver reaches the certified optimum whenever one is available, matches Gurobi's performance on smaller instances, and outperforms it on larger instances, finding lower-energy solutions. These results show that the competitiveness of the hybrid solver on binary quadratic programs, so far observed only on a synthetic benchmark, extends to a real-world problem. This motivates comparisons with classical heuristics specific to protein side-chain optimization.
